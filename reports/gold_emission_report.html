Technical summary

The monetary reconstruction is usable and materially more precise than raw kill counts, but it does not overturn the original economic conclusion. Reliable loot models cover 98.3% of non-boss deaths across 90,701 world-days. Matched fixed-effects coefficients are indistinguishable from zero at the 1-, 7-, and 30-day headline specifications, and the best holdout result improves random-walk RMSE by only 0.14% (Raw kill count, 1 day).

The reconstructed series measures economic scale, not observed sales

The direct-coin line is the narrowest emission estimate. The 50% line adds half of the expected NPC-sale value and therefore remains a scenario, not an observation. Normalizing by average online players makes worlds comparable, while the daily archive coverage field prevents missing loot tables from being mistaken for zero production.

Monetary weighting does not create a stable price signal

At matched horizons, raw kills, direct GP drops, potential emission, and the 50% realization scenario all have small coefficients relative to their clustered uncertainty. Isolated significance elsewhere in the lag grid is not robust across coverage thresholds, boss treatment, realization rates, and holdout evaluation. This supports a descriptive null, not proof that gold supply can never matter.

Holdout forecasts reject the apparent in-sample improvements

A positive bar means lower RMSE than a zero-return random walk. Nearly every monetary specification is at or below zero, and the deterioration grows at 30 days. The appropriate conclusion is that the reconstructed production variables do not add dependable directional forecast information in this sample.

Verdict. Tested directly against later prices, gold emission shows no reliable signal at separately tested delays from 1 to 90 days, and no monetary series beats the random walk out of sample. This is a result about the direct test only. The analysis does not model the intermediate path from GP emission into GP circulation and Tibia Coin turnover, so it cannot reject that economic channel and cannot estimate a GP-to-TC absorption lag. Until that path is identified, the conversion interval and its price effect remain unknown.

Scope, definitions, and economic boundary

  • GP (gold pieces): Tibia's in-game monetary unit.
  • NPC: a non-player character. Only guaranteed prices paid by an NPC to a player count.
  • Loot table: possible drops plus empirical frequency and quantity evidence.
  • Direct emission: nominal Gold, Platinum, and Crystal Coins expected to drop.
  • Potential emission: direct emission plus the maximum guaranteed NPC value of sellable loot.
  • Conservative potential emission: the same, priced only at NPC buyers reachable without a quest or a store purchase. It retains 82.6% of potential emission, which is what the best-buyer-access assumption is worth.
  • Realized emission estimate: direct emission plus 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% of potential NPC sales, plus one scenario using assumed per-item-category sale rates. Realization rates are declared assumptions, not observed quantities.
  • Cumulative emission index: summed modeled flow since each world's first sample date; it is not the true money stock.

Inter-player Market values are zero because they transfer existing GP between accounts. No net-flow or true monetary-stock claim is made because measurable sink data are unavailable.

Canonical creatures and loot values remain auditable

Names are normalized with the kill archive's explicit alias map and stable identifiers. The latest TibiaWiki Loot2 block supplies empirical kills, times dropped, quantities, and item totals. Tables below 100 source kills remain insufficient rather than receiving an invented value. Bosses are identified from TibiaWiki plus the Bosstiary list and retained outside the main series.

High-value examples are mostly low-frequency encounters

The audit table ranks standard, non-boss creatures by potential GP per kill. Large values must be read alongside source sample size and confidence. Across world-days, the largest single-creature contribution reaches only 17.9% at the 99th percentile, so the aggregate result is usually not driven by one creature.

Methodology maps the fourteen requested steps to reproducible outputs

1. Canonicalize every positive kill-stat creature and preserve aliases.

2. Cache complete loot-statistics pages with revision IDs and collection timestamps.

3. Classify coins, NPC-sellable items, and zero-valued player-only items.

4. Compute item and creature expected GP per kill with auditable components.

5. Separate direct, potential, and four realization scenarios.

6. Aggregate world-day flows and cumulative indices.

7. Measure death coverage and flag sub-80% or partial-date observations.

8. Separate bosses and retain event/special-creature flags.

9. Normalize by average online players, active characters, and 1,000 kills.

10. Test 1/7/14/30/60/90-day lags and 7/14/30/60/90-day moving averages.

11. Build cumulative gross-emission indices without calling them true stocks.

12. Reestimate equivalent world/date fixed-effects specifications.

13. Vary realization, coverage, NPC access assumptions, and boss inclusion.

14. Inspect extreme creatures, concentration, and per-player world rankings.

Limitations define what the estimates cannot establish

  • Fan-site probabilities are empirical and revision-specific; rare boss loot remains noisy.
  • NPC buyer metadata does not fully encode travel, faction, reputation, or quest access. The maximum-price scenario is potential; the conservative scenario therefore uses direct GP drops only.
  • Collection and actual NPC sale behavior are unobserved, so realization rates are parameters.
  • Event flags are retained without multiplying loot because event-specific mechanics are not encoded in the source cache.
  • Summoned or training creatures receive zero only with source evidence; unmatched entries remain uncovered.
  • Gold sinks and the initial gold stock are unavailable, preventing a net-stock estimate.
  • Fixed-effects and temporal precedence do not identify causality.

Recommended next steps

1. Collect item pickup and NPC-sale telemetry, if accessible, to estimate realization rates instead of parameterizing them.

2. Build a reviewed NPC-access dimension so the conservative item scenario can include broadly accessible buyers.

3. Measure major sinks—Market fees, blessings, imbuements, travel, and NPC purchases—to construct a net-flow index.

4. Extend the kill archive before drawing conclusions at 60- and 90-day horizons.

5. Monitor coverage and source revisions automatically; do not silently backfill missing creatures.

Further questions

  • Do realization rates vary systematically by item weight, unit value, or world activity?
  • Are event-period emission shocks absorbed through Tibia Coin demand, other sinks, or gold hoarding?
  • Does a measured net-flow index explain the common price level better than gross emission?
  • Can longer history distinguish a slow stock effect from the short-horizon predictive null?
